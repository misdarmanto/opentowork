**AGPL Trends (based on prior reporting, not live search):**

The AGPLv3 remains a niche but strategically significant license. Its defining feature — Section 13, requiring modified source be offered to *network* users — was designed to close the "SaaS loophole" in standard GPL. Adoption spiked ~2018–2022 among open-core startups seeking protection from hyperscalers reselling hosted versions. Notable adopters include Grafana, MinIO, Nextcloud, Element, and GitLab's earlier core.

Key trendlines:

- **Defection to "source-available" licenses**: MongoDB (SSPL), Elastic, Redis (RSAL/AGPL exit) moved away when AGPL was judged too weak or ambiguous, fueling debate over its enforceability.
- **Moderate, not runaway, growth**: AGPL holds a low single-digit share of project licenses; permissive licenses (MIT/Apache) continue dominating per Black Duck annual reports.
- **Compliance ambiguity**: Enterprises often avoid AGPL fearing undefined scope (network boundaries, microservices), limiting corporate adoption.
- **Enduring use case**: Anti-cloud "fair use" hedging and dual-licensing (open-core) strategies keep AGPL relevant among AI and infrastructure startups.
